# Game Archive

[简体中文](README.md) | **English**

A chronological archive of video game hardware and long-running game series.

[Browse the archive in English](https://chihiko3.github.io/GameConsole/?lang=en) | [Report an issue](https://github.com/Chihiko3/GameConsole/issues) | [Contribute](https://github.com/Chihiko3/GameConsole/pulls)

Use the language control at the top of the site to switch between Simplified Chinese and English. Interface copy, category labels, and series terminology have received a manual terminology pass, while long-form prose remains machine-assisted and is still being reviewed entry by entry. English mode displays a prominent unproofread warning until that review is complete.

## What is this?

Video game history rarely follows a clean, straight line. Main entries are often surrounded by spin-offs, remakes, sequels, enhanced editions, and platform migrations. Hardware companies may develop home consoles, handhelds, add-ons, and revised models at the same time. A Wiki page can explain one product in detail, but it is much harder to see where a series branched, which experiments survived, and which releases changed its direction.

Game Archive brings this scattered information back into chronological context. It is designed both as a quick reference for players looking for release order and platform information, and as a deeper research tool for game designers, developers, and anyone studying how a series evolves.

## What can you explore?

- **Unified timelines:** Hardware and game releases are ordered by their earliest known public release anywhere in the world.
- **Chinese and English interface:** Both languages use the same timeline data and interactions. The current English version is explicitly marked as unproofread.
- **Layered reading:** Primary cards identify each release at a glance. Expanded panels provide platform records, core experiences, mechanical changes, series impact, plot summaries, and timeline-specific details.
- **Series overview:** Select multiple game series and compare their releases on one combined timeline.
- **Historical milestones:** Identify important domestic breakthroughs, global breakthroughs, and the first releases following major team or company integrations.
- **Version relationships:** Distinguish main entries, spin-offs, remakes, enhanced editions, sequels, ROM hacks, and later platform releases.
- **Visual references:** Cards display hardware images, cover art, or logos. Entries with multiple images can cycle through them and show a larger preview on hover.

## Current coverage

### Video game hardware

The hardware timeline begins with early systems from Magnavox, Atari, Nintendo, and Sega, then continues through PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, Steam Deck, and other modern platforms. It includes home consoles, handhelds, hybrid systems, handheld PCs, add-ons, hardware revisions, notable launch titles, and representative games.

### Game series

- **Pokémon:** Core series releases, remakes, enhanced versions, and selected notable fan games and ROM hacks.
- **Final Fantasy:** Main entries, direct sequels, the Tactics line, Crystal Chronicles, Chocobo titles, and other releases within the Final Fantasy product lineage.
- **Dragon Quest:** Main entries, remakes, Monsters, Mystery Dungeon, Builders, and other major branches.
- **Xeno Series:** A creative-lineage timeline connecting Xenogears, Xenosaga, and Xenoblade while preserving their separate copyright and setting boundaries.
- **Like a Dragon:** Main entries, historical spin-offs, Kurohyo, Judgment, remakes, and other major side stories.
- **Spike Chunsoft Narrative:** A narrative-game lineage ranging from Otogirisō and Kamaitachi no Yoru to Machi and 428, alongside Zero Escape, Danganronpa, AI: The Somnium Files, and related branches.

The archive is still expanding. Additional series will be added when their research reaches a useful level of completeness.

## How to use the archive

1. Choose **Video Game Hardware** or **Game Series** at the top of the page.
2. Open a series and follow its releases down the vertical timeline.
3. Select a primary card to view deeper interpretation, platform records, and series-specific information.
4. Use **Overview** to select several series and compare their releases within the same period.
5. Hover over card artwork to inspect a larger cover, hardware image, or logo.

## Research approach

Timelines use the earliest known public release date for a game or hardware product anywhere in the world. Dates, platforms, version relationships, and hardware information are cross-checked wherever possible through official websites, publisher materials, reliable Wikis, game databases, historical archives, and other traceable sources.

Sections such as “Core Experience,” “Mechanical Changes,” and “Series Impact” are editorial research. Verifiable facts and analytical interpretation should remain distinct, and a reasonable inference should not be presented as an official design intention. Disputed or weakly documented subjects remain open to correction as better evidence becomes available.

[Read the full research and verification policy](RESEARCH_SOURCES.en.md)

## Contributing

Issues and pull requests are welcome. You can help fill gaps, correct release dates or platform information, improve a series analysis, or provide a more accurate image. For factual corrections, please include a source whenever possible and identify the affected series and release.

When using an AI assistant to add a timeline or modify the project, provide [`AI_PROJECT_GUIDE.en.md`](AI_PROJECT_GUIDE.en.md) first. It records the repository structure, full timeline-integration checklist, and shared rules that must remain intact.

The project is intended to support two levels of reading. Players should be able to answer “What comes first, and where can I play it?” while designers and researchers can continue into “What changed in this release, why is it worth studying, and how did it shape what followed?”
